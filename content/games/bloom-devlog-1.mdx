Most city builders give you a city. You place the roads. You zone the districts. You wait for the number to go up.

BloomGrid is something different. It gives you a world full of people, and then it lets them get on with their lives.

## What It Is

**BloomGrid** is a cozy isometric voxel simulation — part city builder, part life sim, part emergent storytelling engine. The world is alive with autonomous citizens going about their days: eating, working, sleeping, making friends, falling out, building things together. You can watch from a god-view above it all, or drop into third-person and follow a single baker through their morning, or go first-person and sit in the corner of a tavern and just *observe*.

The world doesn't pause when you look away. The baker's day keeps happening. The supply chain connecting the mill to the bakery to the market keeps ticking. The two neighbours who started working at the same smithy are either going to become close friends or quietly stop talking to each other — the simulation will decide, based on how their days have been going.

## Living People

The characters in BloomGrid aren't props. They operate on a full needs system — physiological needs like food and sleep sit at the base, above which layers of social needs and personal purpose accumulate. A character who's well-fed and rested will seek out their friends. One who's exhausted and lonely will make different choices.

Relationships evolve from shared experience. The game tracks the events that matter — when two people first met, what they worked on together, where things went well or badly. These become the raw material for what the simulation calls *Lifelines*: procedurally generated personal histories that shape how characters behave going forward.

A town's personality emerges from the cumulative weight of all those small histories running in parallel.

## The World Engine

Under the social layer sits an economy built on real supply and demand. A bakery can't produce bread unless flour is arriving from a mill. A mill needs grain from farms. Farms need weather. Infrastructure — roads, eventually trains and boats — determines how efficiently the whole system flows.

When a region becomes well-connected and the economy matures, the population grows. A small village becomes a town. A town eventually becomes something more. All of it driven by the collective decisions of simulated people responding to real conditions, not a scripted progression system.

## The Look

The visual language is deliberate: warm golden-hour light, a soft voxel palette that never reads as cold or clinical. The vegetation sways. Windows glow in the evening. The world is small enough in detail to be readable from above and rich enough up close to be worth dropping into.

Think late afternoon light through a village on the edge of a forest. That's the constant.

## Where Things Are

BloomGrid is in its early foundation phase — the ECS, chunk and voxel system, and isometric camera are being built now. The deep simulation systems are designed and documented; the work ahead is building the engine that can carry them.

I'll write more as those pieces come together. The interesting part is knowing exactly what you're building toward and working backward from there — and what we're building toward is a world that could, if you left it long enough, surprise you.
